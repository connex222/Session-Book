How to use this library

Every session follows the same four-part shape, because a drill without an objective is just an activity. Read the objective first and decide whether it is the thing your team actually needs this week. Set the area up as described, run it, and hold your coaching points to the four listed rather than commenting on everything you see.

Objective

What the session is genuinely for. If you cannot say it in one sentence to the players, do not run it.

Setup

Area size, numbers and rules. Dimensions are a starting point — change the space before you change anything else.

Coaching points

The four things worth stopping the practice for. Everything else can wait until the drive home.

Progressions

Three ways to raise the difficulty once the group has it. Change one variable at a time.

Football training drills — common questions

How long should a football training session be?

Most grassroots and semi-professional sessions run between 60 and 90 minutes. A workable shape is 10 minutes of warm-up, 15 to 20 minutes of technical work, 20 to 25 minutes on the main practice and 15 to 20 minutes of a game. Every drill here is designed to sit inside one of those blocks rather than fill the whole night.

How many players do I need for these drills?

The small group sessions work with as few as four to eight players. The rondos and technical circuits suit six to twelve. The phase-of-play and team-shape practices want sixteen or more. Each session lists its numbers in the setup, so you can pick by whoever turned up.

What size area should I set up?

Every session gives dimensions in the setup, and they are a starting point rather than a rule. If the practice is too easy, shrink the area or add a defender. If players cannot complete three passes, make it bigger before you change anything else.

How do I make a drill harder or easier?

Each session lists three progressions. In general: reduce the space, reduce the touches, add a defender, add a time limit, or add a consequence for losing the ball. Reverse any of those to make it easier. Change one thing at a time so you can see what caused the difference.

What do the diagrams mean?

Blue circles are the team in possession and white circles are the opposition or defenders. A dashed line is a pass and a solid orange line is a run or a dribble. Numbers refer to shirt positions, so the 6 is the holding midfielder and the 9 is the centre forward.

Are there goalkeeping drills?

Yes — five of them, covering shot-stopping, dealing with crosses, one-v-ones, distribution under pressure and sweeper-keeping. Filter by Goalkeeping to find them.

Are these drills free to use?

Yes. Every session can be printed, shared or run with your own squad at no cost. Use the share buttons on any session to send it to a coach on WhatsApp or by email.
