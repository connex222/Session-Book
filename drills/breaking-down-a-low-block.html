Breaking Down a Low Block

In Possession • Attacking Third • 10v8

By Shaun Ellison · FA Level 2 · Assistant Coach, Turton FCPublished August 2026 · updated August 2026

Create and exploit openings against a deep, compact defence without losing the structure needed to defend the counter that follows.

Setup

  • Final third with the defending team in a deep 4-4 block and two goalkeepers
  • Ten attackers against eight defenders, with two target zones behind the block
  • One point for a shot inside the box, two for a shot following an entry into the pocket or the space behind

Key coaching points

  • Move the block before trying to break it — high-tempo circulation comes first
  • Occupy what the block cannot cover: the half-spaces, the pocket, the space behind the full-back
  • Disguise entry passes, and recirculate rather than force a pass that isn't there
  • Third-man runs and cutbacks beat crosses against a deep block — get behind them, not over them

Progressions

  • Cap each possession at 90 seconds to raise the tempo
  • Allow the block to counter into the target zones so rest defence is tested
  • Remove one attacker so the numerical superiority is less comfortable

What the diagram shows

Ten attackers against eight defenders who are set deep and compact, with the pitch marked into build, progress and penetrate zones. The dashed lines show the ball being circulated wide, then switched quickly across to the opposite side, and the orange arrows show two runners attacking the space that opens as the block slides across. The gap being exploited is not in front of the defence — it appears at the far side of it, and only after the block has been made to move.

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the switch. The block has to be pulled across before there is anything to attack.
  • Everybody staying in front of the ball, so there is nothing to aim at when a gap does appear.
  • Crossing from deep because it feels like progress. A deep cross into a packed box is a turnover with extra steps.
  • Forgetting rest defence. Against a low block you will lose the ball in their half — be set up for it.

What good looks like

  • The ball changes sides at least twice before a genuine chance is created.
  • At least two players attack the box on every entry, arriving from different angles.
  • Patience in the build zone and real speed in the penetrate zone — not the same tempo throughout.

Age groups and numbers

Under-15s and above, and open-age in particular — it is the problem every decent team faces most weeks. It needs enough players to be realistic, so it is not worth running below about sixteen. If numbers are short, use the 4v4 corner-goal possession game instead to coach the switch in isolation.
