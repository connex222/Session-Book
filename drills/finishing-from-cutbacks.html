Finishing from Cutbacks

In Possession • Attacking Third • Finishing

By Shaun Ellison · FA Level 2 · Assistant Coach, Turton FCPublished August 2026 · updated August 2026

Diagram

Objective

Convert the most common chance in the game — the cutback from a wide area — with clean, first-time finishes from repeatable positions.

Setup

  • Penalty area with a goalkeeper, a wide server on each side and a queue of finishers centrally
  • Server drives a cutback along the ground to the penalty spot or the edge of the six-yard box
  • Finishers arrive from central and far-post positions in turn

Key coaching points

  • Attack the ball on the move — never stand and wait for the cutback to arrive
  • Get the body over the ball and strike through it with the laces or inside of the foot
  • Keep the finish low and across the goalkeeper unless the near post is clearly open
  • A second runner covers the far post for anything across the face of goal

Common mistakes

  • Running towards the ball rather than across it, which kills the angle and the pace.
  • Trying to place the finish. From six yards a firm contact through the middle of the ball beats a clever one nine times out of ten.
  • Arriving early and then standing. Time the run so you meet the ball on the move.
  • Servers cutting the ball back too hard or behind the runner. The service is half the practice — coach it.

What good looks like

  • The striker is moving as the ball is struck, never before.
  • Finishes are first time and kept below the crossbar.
  • Both feet are being used, because the side of the service decides it.

Coach's language

  • “Can you create the advantage first?”
  • “Stretch them.”
  • “Arrive, don’t wait.”
  • “Now — attack the space.”

Progressions

  • Alternate the service side without warning
  • Add a recovering defender to pressure the finisher
  • Add a live wide 1v1 before the cutback so the service has to be earned

Age groups and numbers

Under-10s upwards. For younger players, remove the goalkeeper and slow the service so the technique can be grooved. For older groups, add a defender on the striker’s shoulder — finishing under contact is a different skill entirely.

What the diagram shows

Servers on both sides of the penalty area with a striker central and a goalkeeper in goal. The dashed lines show cutbacks arriving from either side — pulled back from the byline rather than crossed from deep — and the orange arrow shows the striker’s movement: a check away from the ball, then a hard move back across the six-yard line to meet it. The finish is taken first time. Nobody in the picture is standing still waiting for service.

Coach's approach
