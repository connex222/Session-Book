Possession Rondo – Secure & Progress

In Possession • Middle Third • 5v2

By Shaun Ellison · FA Level 2 · Assistant Coach, Turton FCPublished August 2026 · updated August 2026

Secure possession under pressure and progress the ball through the two defenders using quick, purposeful combinations.

Setup

  • 12m x 12m square
  • 5 attackers on the outside plus 1 floater inside vs. 2 defenders
  • Point scored for a pass that splits the two defenders into the floater

Key coaching points

  • First touch played away from the nearest defender
  • Body shape open before the ball arrives — always play forwards when possible
  • Communicate early: call for the ball before it's played
  • Quick 1‑2 touch combinations rather than holding the ball

Progressions

  • Reduce to a strict 2-touch maximum
  • Add a second floater to increase central options
  • Defenders score a point if they win the ball or force it out

What the diagram shows

Five players form a ring around the outside of a twelve-metre square, with one floater working inside it and two defenders hunting the ball. In the picture the ball has just been played from the bottom-left outside player up to the top of the square, and the receiving player is turning to feed the floater between the two defenders. That vertical pass — the one that goes through the middle rather than around the edge — is the whole point of the exercise. Passes around the outside keep possession; the pass into the floater breaks a line.

Common mistakes

  • Players standing still and passing around the square. If the ball can travel in a straight line to you, you are in the wrong place — move a yard either way and make the angle.
  • Taking the first touch towards the nearest defender, which turns two touches into a turnover.
  • The floater hiding behind a defender instead of moving into the passing lane. The floater should be the busiest player in the square.
  • Coaches counting passes. Count passes into the floater instead — that is the behaviour you actually want.

What good looks like

  • The ball moves faster than the two defenders can shift across.
  • Every third or fourth pass goes into the floater rather than around the ring.
  • Players call for the ball before it is played to them, not as it arrives.

Age groups and numbers

Works from under-11s upwards. Younger groups should start at 6v2 in a larger square with no touch limit; add the two-touch rule only once they can complete ten passes. With open-age players, shrink the square rather than adding defenders — less space produces the same pressure with far more repetitions.
