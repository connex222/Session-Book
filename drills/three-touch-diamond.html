Session 36 of 50

Three-Touch Diamond

Technical · Three Touch · Groups of 4

By Shaun Ellison · FA Level 2 · Assistant Coach, Turton FCPublished August 2026 · updated August 2026

Diagram

Objective

Build a clean three-touch habit — kill it, set it, play it — then strip the touches away one at a time until the group is circulating first-time.

Setup

  • 12m x 12m diamond, one player at each corner, one ball per group of four
  • Start on three touches: first touch out of the feet, second to set, third to pass
  • Rotate the direction of circulation every 90 seconds

Key coaching points

  • First touch goes into the space you intend to pass into, never straight down
  • Second touch sets the ball to a comfortable striking distance
  • Head up on the second touch, not the third — the picture is taken before the pass
  • Receive on the half-turn so the next player is already in your eyeline

Common mistakes

  • Players complete the movement without quality in the first touch or pass.
  • The drill becomes a queue rather than a continuous practice.
  • Speed is increased before technique is reliable.

What good looks like

  • The technical action remains clean as speed and pressure increase.
  • Players prepare the next action before receiving.
  • There is enough repetition for the behaviour to become automatic.

Coach's language

  • “Quality first, then speed.”
  • “What is your next action?”
  • “Check before you receive.”
  • “Can you make the next one better?”

Progressions

  • Drop to two touches once the group completes 20 clean circuits
  • Drop to one touch and count the longest unbroken sequence
  • Add a second ball travelling the opposite way around the diamond

Age groups and numbers

Best fit: All ages · Group: 2–8

Use it as a technical block or arrival practice; quality matters more than speed.

Shaun's coaching eye

I would avoid turning this into a passing exercise for its own sake. The technical action matters because it helps the player see the next pass, move away from pressure or progress the team.

If they struggled…

If players are struggling, reduce the pressure and make the receiving picture clearer. Coach the body shape and first action before demanding speed.

If they mastered it…

If they are comfortable, increase the pressure or add a forward target. The next challenge should make the same technique serve a bigger decision.

Session rhythm

Coach's approach

Coach the detail that changes the game, then get the players back playing. If the practice is not working, change the space before adding another rule.
