Baiting the Press – Inviting Pressure to Break It

In Possession • Defensive Third • Manipulation

By Shaun Ellison · FA Level 2 · Assistant Coach, Turton FCPublished August 2026 · updated August 2026

Diagram

Objective

Deliberately invite the opposition to press, then use the space their press vacates — a controlled risk executed with conviction.

Setup

  • Defensive and middle thirds, building against a high-pressing opposition
  • One free player and the goalkeeper are always available behind the first line
  • A point for playing through the press into a target striker; minus a point for a turnover in the build zone

Key coaching points

  • The invitation has to look real: receive facing your own goal and let the presser commit
  • The escape pass is decided before the ball arrives — hesitation turns bait into a mistake
  • The pass after breaking the press goes forward and fast; the space closes in seconds
  • Everyone accepts the risk: full-backs stay high and wide to stretch the press, not to help it

Common mistakes

  • One player presses alone while the rest of the unit stays disconnected.
  • The press starts without a clear trigger.
  • Players chase the ball and open the next pass.

What good looks like

  • The first presser forces play towards a planned area.
  • Cover and communication arrive early enough to make the next pass uncomfortable.
  • Players recognise the trigger rather than pressing simply because the ball moves.

Coach's language

  • “What is our trigger?”
  • “Press the ball, cover the next pass.”
  • “Can we make the pitch smaller?”
  • “Don’t chase — trap.”

Progressions

  • Shrink the build zone so the margin for error is smaller
  • Remove the free player so the goalkeeper has to be the spare man
  • Make a turnover in the build zone a live counter against the team

Age groups and numbers

Best fit: U14–Adult · Group: 8–22+

Increase realism by linking the practice to the next phase rather than simply adding constraints.

Coach's eye

Don't coach everything at once. Pick the one or two behaviours that matter most, then change the space or pressure before adding more rules.
