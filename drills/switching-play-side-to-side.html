Switching Play – Side to Side

In Possession • Middle Third • 8v8

By Shaun Ellison · FA Level 2 · Assistant Coach, Turton FCPublished August 2026 · updated August 2026

Diagram

Objective

Move the opposition from one side of the pitch to the other, and recognise the moment the switch is actually worth playing.

Setup

  • Full width area with two marked wide channels and a central zone
  • 8v8, with one attacker permanently stationed in each wide channel
  • A point for every switch that reaches the far channel in three passes or fewer

Key coaching points

  • Draw the opposition across first — a switch without a shift achieved is a wasted pass
  • Play the switch through the free player at the base rather than a risky long diagonal
  • Wide player checks their shoulder and takes the first touch forward, into space
  • Central players rotate as the ball travels so the far side is never isolated

Common mistakes

  • Players stand behind defenders instead of creating clear passing lines.
  • The ball moves faster than the support, leaving the receiver isolated.
  • Players force a forward pass when the better decision is to secure possession and move the opponent.

What good looks like

  • The player on the ball has at least two useful options.
  • Support arrives at useful angles and distances, not simply close to the ball.
  • Players recognise when to play forward and when to recycle.

Coach's language

  • “Can you give me two options?”
  • “Open your body before the ball arrives.”
  • “Can we move them before we play through?”
  • “Secure it, then progress.”

Progressions

  • Double points for a switch played first-time
  • Restrict the wide players to two touches
  • Add goals so every successful switch ends in an attempt

Age groups and numbers

Best fit: U11–U16 · Group: 6–18+

Use the stated numbers as the base and adjust space before adding complexity.

Shaun's coaching eye

I would avoid turning this into a passing exercise for its own sake. The technical action matters because it helps the player see the next pass, move away from pressure or progress the team.

Why I use this

If they struggled…

If players are struggling, reduce the pressure and make the receiving picture clearer. Coach the body shape and first action before demanding speed.

If they mastered it…

If they are comfortable, increase the pressure or add a forward target. The next challenge should make the same technique serve a bigger decision.
