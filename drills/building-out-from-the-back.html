Building Out From the Back

In Possession • Defensive Third • Rondo Overload

By Shaun Ellison · FA Level 2 · Assistant Coach, Turton FCPublished August 2026 · updated August 2026

Improve composure and decision-making when playing through pressure from the goalkeeper and back line, using the GK as an auxiliary spare man.

Setup

  • 20m x 15m area split into a Build Zone and a Progression Zone
  • GK + 2 centre-backs + 1 holding midfielder vs. 1 presser in the Build Zone
  • Two free outlets waiting in the Progression Zone

Key coaching points

  • Open body shape to receive on the half-turn
  • GK treated as an extra passing option, not just a safety valve
  • Scan for the presser's angle before the ball arrives
  • Play forward the moment a passing lane opens — don't force it sideways forever

Progressions

  • Add a second presser once the group completes 8/10 clean exits
  • Add a 5-second limit on the ball in the Build Zone
  • Condition: a pass into the Progression Zone must be on the ground

What the diagram shows

A twenty by fifteen metre area split into a Build Zone and a Progression Zone. The goalkeeper, two centre-backs and a holding midfielder work in the Build Zone against a single presser, with two free outlets waiting in the Progression Zone. The dashed lines trace one successful exit: keeper to the right centre-back, who has opened their body to receive, then a longer pass into the outlet ahead. The presser is deliberately positioned between the centre-back and the holding midfielder — the pass everybody wants to play is the one that has been taken away.

Common mistakes

  • Centre-backs receiving with their toes pointing at their own goal, which makes forward play impossible before the ball even arrives.
  • Treating the goalkeeper as a last resort. The keeper is a spare player — use them to change the angle and make the presser run again.
  • Passing sideways forever because it feels safe. Sideways passes do not eliminate anybody.
  • Playing the forward pass before the presser has committed. Wait for them to move, then go past them.

What good looks like

  • The presser is made to run at least twice before the ball leaves the Build Zone.
  • Eight out of ten repetitions end with a pass into the Progression Zone on the ground.
  • Players are receiving on the half-turn without being reminded.

Age groups and numbers

From under-11s, when goalkeepers first become genuine outfield options. Younger groups should run it unopposed until the shape and body positions are automatic. For open-age teams, add the second presser immediately — one presser is not a realistic picture of a Saturday afternoon.
