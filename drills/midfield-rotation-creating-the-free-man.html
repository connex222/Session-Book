Midfield Rotation – Creating the Free Man

In Possession • Middle Third • Rotations

By Shaun Ellison · FA Level 2 · Assistant Coach, Turton FCPublished August 2026 · updated August 2026

Diagram

Objective

Use simple midfield rotations to unbalance the opposition's markers and consistently create a free player to receive.

Setup

  • 35m x 30m central area with a marked pivot zone in front of the defence
  • Two centre-backs plus a pivot and two number 8s against four pressing opponents
  • The team in possession scores by playing through to a target player beyond the area

Key coaching points

  • One player drops as another pushes on — never both, and never neither
  • The rotation only works if it drags a marker; move to take an opponent with you
  • Whoever ends up free shows and calls for the ball immediately
  • Receive facing the direction of the next pass whenever the picture allows

Common mistakes

  • Players stand behind defenders instead of creating clear passing lines.
  • The ball moves faster than the support, leaving the receiver isolated.
  • Players force a forward pass when the better decision is to secure possession and move the opponent.

What good looks like

  • The player on the ball has at least two useful options.
  • Support arrives at useful angles and distances, not simply close to the ball.
  • Players recognise when to play forward and when to recycle.

Coach's language

  • “Can you give me two options?”
  • “Open your body before the ball arrives.”
  • “Can we move them before we play through?”
  • “Secure it, then progress.”

Progressions

  • Add a fifth presser so the rotation is harder to execute
  • Condition that the pass to the target must follow a completed rotation
  • Reduce the area to increase the speed of thought required

Age groups and numbers

Best fit: U11–U16 · Group: 6–18+

Use the stated numbers as the base and adjust space before adding complexity.

Coach's eye

Don't coach everything at once. Pick the one or two behaviours that matter most, then change the space or pressure before adding more rules.
