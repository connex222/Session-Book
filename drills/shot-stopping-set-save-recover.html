Shot-Stopping — Set, Save, Recover

Shot-Stopping · Reaction Saves

By Shaun Ellison · FA Level 2 · Assistant Coach, Turton FCPublished August 2026 · updated August 2026

Build the habit of being set before every strike, saving to a safe area, and recovering to the feet fast enough to deal with the rebound.

Setup

  • Penalty area with three servers arranged across the edge of the box
  • Servers strike in sequence, two to three seconds apart, alternating sides
  • The goalkeeper must be set — feet still, weight forward — before each strike

Key coaching points

  • Be set as the server plants their standing foot, not as they make contact
  • Save into the safe area — wide of the post or back over the bar, never straight out
  • Recover to the feet immediately; the second ball is the one that punishes you
  • Hands lead the body on low saves; do not drop the shoulder first

Progressions

  • Reduce the gap between strikes to one second to force faster recovery
  • Add a striker on the six-yard line to attack every rebound
  • Serve from wider angles so the starting position has to change each time

What the diagram shows

Three servers spread across the edge of the penalty area with the goalkeeper in goal. The dashed lines show strikes arriving in sequence from left, centre and right, two to three seconds apart, and the orange arrow shows the keeper’s lateral adjustment between saves. The keeper has to be set — feet still, weight forward — before each strike, which means the recovery from the previous save has to be quick enough to allow it. The practice is as much about the movement between shots as the shots themselves.

Common mistakes

  • Still moving when the ball is struck. Being set beats being in the perfect position.
  • Saving straight back into the danger area. Push wide of the post or over the bar.
  • Staying down after the save. The rebound is what punishes goalkeepers at every level.
  • Dropping the shoulder before the hands on low saves, which adds time you do not have.

What good looks like

  • Feet are still at the moment of every contact.
  • Saves finish wide or high, not in front of goal.
  • The keeper is back on their feet and set before the next server strikes.

Age groups and numbers

From under-10s upwards, with the serving distance and pace adjusted to the age. Younger keepers should work with two servers and a four second gap. For older or open-age keepers, cut the gap to one second and add a striker attacking every rebound — the fatigue is part of the point.
