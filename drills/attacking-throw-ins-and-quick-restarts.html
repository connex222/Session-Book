Attacking Throw-Ins & Quick Restarts

Set Pieces • Attacking • Restarts

By Shaun Ellison · FA Level 2 · Assistant Coach, Turton FCPublished August 2026 · updated August 2026

Diagram

Objective

Treat every throw-in and restart as an attacking opportunity, with rehearsed movement and a habit of restarting quickly.

Setup

  • Wide area of the final third with a thrower, four attackers and three defenders
  • Three rehearsed patterns: the short set-back, the flick down the line, and the switch inside
  • The coach calls the pattern at first, then leaves the group to choose

Key coaching points

  • Restart quickly — the advantage lives in the first three seconds, not the tenth
  • One player always shows short to give the thrower a guaranteed option
  • Movement is created before the throw: someone goes away so someone else can come towards
  • The receiver's first touch goes forward, or the throw has achieved nothing

Common mistakes

  • Players learn the movement without understanding the trigger.
  • The restart becomes predictable because the timing is too slow.
  • The team forgets its defensive security when the first option is blocked.

What good looks like

  • Players know their starting positions and the trigger for movement.
  • The routine creates a clear first advantage rather than relying on a perfect execution.
  • Players can continue the attack if the first option is unavailable.

Coach's language

  • “Know your starting picture.”
  • “Wait for the trigger.”
  • “Sell the first movement.”
  • “If option one is blocked, what is next?”

Progressions

  • Add a fourth defender so the patterns have to be genuinely disguised
  • Condition that the ball must be moved forward within two touches of the throw
  • Extend into a live 6v5 attack for 20 seconds after every restart

Age groups and numbers

Best fit: U11–U16 · Group: 6–18+

Use the stated numbers as the base and adjust space before adding complexity.

Coach's eye

Don't coach everything at once. Pick the one or two behaviours that matter most, then change the space or pressure before adding more rules.
