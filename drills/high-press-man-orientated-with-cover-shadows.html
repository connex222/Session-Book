Session 32 of 50

High Press – Man-Orientated with Cover Shadows

Out of Possession • Attacking Third • 11v11

By Shaun Ellison · FA Level 2 · Assistant Coach, Turton FCPublished August 2026 · updated August 2026

Diagram

Objective

Press the opposition's build-up with man-orientated pressure while using cover shadows to eliminate the pass they most want to play.

Setup

  • Full pitch, opposition building from a goal kick in their own defensive third
  • The defending team presses with a designated first presser and man-orientation behind
  • The press must produce a regain in the opposition's half, or the team drops into a mid-block

Key coaching points

  • The first presser sets the angle; everyone behind takes their reference from that angle, not the ball
  • Cover shadows kill the pass into midfield — press with the body positioned to block, not just to arrive
  • Nearest players mark touch-tight and the back line pushes up to keep the team connected
  • If the press is beaten the whole team drops together — no isolated pressers left upfield

Common mistakes

  • One player presses alone while the rest of the unit stays disconnected.
  • The press starts without a clear trigger.
  • Players chase the ball and open the next pass.

What good looks like

  • The first presser forces play towards a planned area.
  • Cover and communication arrive early enough to make the next pass uncomfortable.
  • Players recognise the trigger rather than pressing simply because the ball moves.

Coach's language

  • “What is our trigger?”
  • “Press the ball, cover the next pass.”
  • “Can we make the pitch smaller?”
  • “Don’t chase — trap.”

Progressions

  • Allow the opposition a long option so the back line must defend the ball over the top
  • Condition the press to produce a regain within eight seconds or drop off
  • Rehearse against the specific build-up shape of the next opponent

Age groups and numbers

Best fit: U14–Adult · Group: 8–22+

Increase realism by linking the practice to the next phase rather than simply adding constraints.

Shaun's coaching eye

I would keep the coaching centred on distances, body shape and the next danger. Stop the practice only when a clear behaviour needs correcting, then let the players solve the next repetition.

Why I use this

I would keep the coaching centred on distances, body shape and the next danger. Stop the practice only when a clear behaviour needs correcting, then let the players solve the next repetition.

If they struggled…

If the defensive behaviour is breaking down, make the space simpler before adding more rules. Give the nearest defender one clear job and build the support around it.

If they mastered it…

If the behaviour is reliable, add a second threat or allow the attack more freedom. The progression should test the same principle under more realistic pressure.
