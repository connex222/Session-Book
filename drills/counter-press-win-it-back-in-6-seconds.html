Counter-Press – Win It Back in 6 Seconds

Out of Possession • Gegenpressing

By Shaun Ellison · FA Level 2 · Assistant Coach, Turton FCPublished August 2026 · updated August 2026

Diagram

Objective

React instantly to losing the ball by counter-pressing in numbers around the loss, aiming to regain possession before the opposition can build a counter-attack.

Setup

  • 30m x 30m zone, possession game (e.g. 6v6) that becomes live the instant the ball is lost
  • A visible or called '6-second' countdown starts at the moment of turnover
  • Failing to regain within 6 seconds means the team resets into a defined defensive shape instead

Key coaching points

  • Nearest 3 players converge on the ball immediately — cut off the easiest out-balls first
  • Show aggression in the first 2 seconds; passivity in that window is the biggest error
  • Players furthest away sprint to close passing lanes, not just to the ball
  • If the 6 seconds elapse, the switch to defensive shape must be immediate and organised

Common mistakes

  • Players react too slowly when possession changes.
  • The first action is always forward, even when the ball is not secure.
  • Players attack without protecting against the counter.

What good looks like

  • The first three seconds after the turnover have a clear purpose.
  • Players either counter quickly or secure the ball and reorganise.
  • The nearest players react first while the rest of the team supports the transition.

Coach's language

  • “First three seconds!”
  • “Can we go forward?”
  • “If it’s not on, secure it.”
  • “React before you think.”

Progressions

  • Reward regaining the ball inside the box with a direct shooting opportunity
  • Reduce the countdown to 4 seconds for a more experienced group
  • Add a numerical disadvantage (e.g. 6v7) to sharpen urgency further

Age groups and numbers

Best fit: U14–Adult · Group: 8–22+

Increase realism by linking the practice to the next phase rather than simply adding constraints.

Coach's eye

Don't coach everything at once. Pick the one or two behaviours that matter most, then change the space or pressure before adding more rules.
