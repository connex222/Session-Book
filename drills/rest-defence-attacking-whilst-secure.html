Rest Defence – Attacking Whilst Secure

In Possession & Transition • Team Structure

By Shaun Ellison · FA Level 2 · Assistant Coach, Turton FCPublished August 2026 · updated August 2026

Diagram

Objective

Attack with numbers while keeping the structure behind the ball that kills the opposition counter before it begins.

Setup

  • Three-quarter pitch, full width: the attacking team builds against a mid-block with two counter zones
  • The attacking team must hold a defined rest-defence structure — typically a back three plus the pivot
  • The defending team scores by reaching either counter zone within eight seconds of a regain

Key coaching points

  • The rest-defence line stays balanced and level with the ball, not deep and flat
  • The holding midfielder screens the space in front of the back line and never joins the box
  • The far-side full-back tucks in as the ball travels — the counter usually comes back that way
  • Counter-press first, retreat second: the nearest three players close the regain instantly

Common mistakes

  • Players react too slowly when possession changes.
  • The first action is always forward, even when the ball is not secure.
  • Players attack without protecting against the counter.

What good looks like

  • The first three seconds after the turnover have a clear purpose.
  • Players either counter quickly or secure the ball and reorganise.
  • The nearest players react first while the rest of the team supports the transition.

Coach's language

  • “First three seconds!”
  • “Can we go forward?”
  • “If it’s not on, secure it.”
  • “React before you think.”

Progressions

  • Increase the counter reward so the balance genuinely matters
  • Add a third counter zone centrally to punish a poor screen
  • Play a full 11v11 phase where every regain becomes a live counter

Age groups and numbers

Best fit: U14–Adult · Group: 8–22+

Increase realism by linking the practice to the next phase rather than simply adding constraints.

Coach's eye

Don't coach everything at once. Pick the one or two behaviours that matter most, then change the space or pressure before adding more rules.
