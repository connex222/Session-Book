Defending as a Pair – Press & Cover

Out of Possession • Defensive Third • 2v2

By Shaun Ellison · FA Level 2 · Assistant Coach, Turton FCPublished August 2026 · updated August 2026

Diagram

Objective

Coordinate the two most basic defensive roles — the presser and the cover — so the pair defends as a unit rather than as two individuals.

Setup

  • 25m x 18m area with a goal and goalkeeper at one end, two cone gates at the other
  • Two attackers play against two defenders who start level with each other
  • Defenders score by winning the ball and dribbling through either gate

Key coaching points

  • Ball-near defender presses to delay, angled to force the attacker away from goal
  • Ball-far defender drops to cover: close enough to help, wide enough to see both
  • Say the roles out loud — “press” and “I've got cover” — every repetition
  • Swap roles the instant the ball is switched; the cover becomes the presser

Common mistakes

  • One player presses alone while the rest of the unit stays disconnected.
  • The press starts without a clear trigger.
  • Players chase the ball and open the next pass.

What good looks like

  • The first presser forces play towards a planned area.
  • Cover and communication arrive early enough to make the next pass uncomfortable.
  • Players recognise the trigger rather than pressing simply because the ball moves.

Coach's language

  • “What is our trigger?”
  • “Press the ball, cover the next pass.”
  • “Can we make the pitch smaller?”
  • “Don’t chase — trap.”

Progressions

  • Make it 3v2 to stretch the pair and force earlier decisions
  • Condition the pair to win the ball within 8 seconds
  • Widen the area so a switch of play tests the recovery of both defenders

Age groups and numbers

Best fit: U8–U12 · Group: 4–16+

Keep the area generous and reduce pressure before adding extra rules.

Shaun's coaching eye

I would keep the coaching centred on distances, body shape and the next danger. Stop the practice only when a clear behaviour needs correcting, then let the players solve the next repetition.

Why I use this

I would keep the coaching centred on distances, body shape and the next danger. Stop the practice only when a clear behaviour needs correcting, then let the players solve the next repetition.

If they struggled…

If the defensive behaviour is breaking down, make the space simpler before adding more rules. Give the nearest defender one clear job and build the support around it.

If they mastered it…

If the behaviour is reliable, add a second threat or allow the attack more freedom. The progression should test the same principle under more realistic pressure.
