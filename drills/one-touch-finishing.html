One-Touch Finishing

Attacking Third · One Touch · Finishing

By Shaun Ellison · FA Level 2 · Assistant Coach, Turton FCPublished August 2026 · updated August 2026

Diagram

Objective

Finish first-time from both sides under a one-touch rule, so the striker learns to set their body before the ball arrives rather than after.

Setup

  • Penalty-area practice with a goalkeeper and a server on each side
  • The striker checks away and back, then finishes the service first-time
  • Alternate the side of the service every repetition

Key coaching points

  • Set the feet and open the body while the ball is still travelling
  • Decide near post or far post before the service arrives, not on contact
  • Contact through the middle of the ball to keep the finish down
  • Move after every finish — reset with a check away, never stand still

Common mistakes

  • The final action is rushed before the advantage has been created.
  • Players attack the same space instead of stretching the defence.
  • The supporting player arrives too early and removes the passing option.

What good looks like

  • Players create a clear advantage before attacking the target area.
  • Runs have purpose and timing rather than simply movement for movement’s sake.
  • The final action is taken at speed once the opportunity appears.

Coach's language

  • “Can you create the advantage first?”
  • “Stretch them.”
  • “Arrive, don’t wait.”
  • “Now — attack the space.”

Progressions

  • Add a passive defender on the striker’s shoulder, then make them live
  • Serve the ball in the air so the finish is a volley or a header
  • Play two strikers who must both be involved before any finish counts

Age groups and numbers

Best fit: All ages · Group: 2–8

Use it as a technical block or arrival practice; quality matters more than speed.

Coach's eye

Don't coach everything at once. Pick the one or two behaviours that matter most, then change the space or pressure before adding more rules.

Session rhythm

Coach's approach
