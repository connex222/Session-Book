1v1 Defending – Force Outside

Out of Possession • Defensive Third • Channel Practice

By Shaun Ellison · FA Level 2 · Assistant Coach, Turton FCPublished August 2026 · updated August 2026

Diagram

Objective

Improve individual defending technique — body position, delaying, and forcing the attacker away from goal.

Setup

  • 15m x 10m channel with a small target goal on the end line
  • Attacker starts with the ball facing a defender who starts goal-side
  • Defender's job: delay and force play down the channel, away from goal

Key coaching points

  • Show the attacker outside — protect the inside line to goal
  • Stay on the toes, small controlled steps, don't dive in early
  • Jockey and delay until support arrives or the moment to win the ball appears
  • Win the ball at the right moment — not the first moment

Common mistakes

  • Diving in on the first touch. The moment you commit, the decision belongs to the attacker.
  • Approaching square, which leaves both sides open and invites the attacker to go inside.
  • Getting too tight too early, so a simple touch past you ends the duel.
  • Watching the ball instead of the hips. The hips tell you where they are going; the ball can lie to you.

What good looks like

  • The attacker ends up in the corner of the channel with nowhere useful to go.
  • The defender is on their toes with small steps, never crossing their feet.
  • The tackle, when it comes, arrives at the second or third touch rather than the first.

Coach's language

  • “Delay first.”
  • “Show them where we want them to go.”
  • “See ball and player.”
  • “Can you arrive under control?”

Progressions

  • Add a second attacker to make it 2v1 (overlap)
  • Narrow the channel to force quicker decisions
  • Add a covering defender to practise communication between the two

Age groups and numbers

Suits every age from under-9s upwards and it is worth returning to constantly — this is the single most transferable defensive habit in the game. With younger players, widen the channel and remove the goal so they are only learning to delay. With older ones, narrow it and add a supporting defender so communication becomes part of the practice.

What the diagram shows

A fifteen by ten metre channel with a small goal on the end line. The attacker starts with the ball facing a defender who is already goal-side. The orange arrow shows the defender approaching on an angle rather than head-on, closing the inside line so the only route left runs down the channel and away from goal. The attacker’s dribble is shown being pushed wide — the defender has not tackled anything, and that is the intended outcome.

Session rhythm

Coach's approach
