One-Touch Wall Passing

In Possession · One Touch · Circuit

By Shaun Ellison · FA Level 2 · Assistant Coach, Turton FCPublished August 2026 · updated August 2026

Diagram

Objective

Play and move at full speed through a one-touch circuit, so the give-and-go becomes automatic rather than something that has to be decided on.

Setup

  • 30m x 20m channel with two static wall players and a finishing goal
  • The runner plays a one-touch pass into each wall player and takes the return in stride
  • Wall players are restricted to one touch throughout

Key coaching points

  • The wall player returns the ball into the space ahead, never back to the feet
  • The runner never stops — the pass is made on the move and the return is met on the move
  • Open the hips before the ball arrives so one touch is actually possible
  • Weight matters more than power; an over-hit return kills the sequence

Common mistakes

  • Players stand behind defenders instead of creating clear passing lines.
  • The ball moves faster than the support, leaving the receiver isolated.
  • Players force a forward pass when the better decision is to secure possession and move the opponent.

What good looks like

  • The player on the ball has at least two useful options.
  • Support arrives at useful angles and distances, not simply close to the ball.
  • Players recognise when to play forward and when to recycle.

Coach's language

  • “Can you give me two options?”
  • “Open your body before the ball arrives.”
  • “Can we move them before we play through?”
  • “Secure it, then progress.”

Progressions

  • Add a passive defender between the two walls, then make them live
  • Time the circuit and ask each pair to beat their own best
  • Finish first-time only — a controlled shot does not count

Age groups and numbers

Best fit: All ages · Group: 2–8

Use it as a technical block or arrival practice; quality matters more than speed.

Coach's eye

Don't coach everything at once. Pick the one or two behaviours that matter most, then change the space or pressure before adding more rules.
