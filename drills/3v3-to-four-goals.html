3v3 to Four Goals

Transition · Continuous · 3v3

By Shaun Ellison · FA Level 2 · Assistant Coach, Turton FCPublished August 2026 · updated August 2026

Force constant scanning by giving each team two goals to attack and two to protect, so the picture changes every time the ball turns over.

Setup

  • 30m x 26m area with two small goals on each end line
  • 3v3, continuous play, balls fed in immediately whenever one goes out
  • A goal in either target counts the same

Key coaching points

  • Scan for the unguarded goal before you receive, not after
  • Switch the point of attack the moment the defenders commit to one side
  • On losing the ball, the nearest player delays and the other two split the two goals
  • Play three-minute blocks at full intensity, then rest properly

Progressions

  • Award double for a goal scored within five seconds of a turnover
  • Add a neutral who always plays for the team in possession
  • Widen the area so the switch is longer and the recovery runs harder

What the diagram shows

A thirty by twenty-six metre area with two small goals on each end line, three players a side. The dashed lines show the ball being moved away from pressure, and the orange arrow shows a run at the goal the defenders have just left unguarded. With two targets at each end, the defending team can never cover both, so the attacking decision is always about which goal is open rather than whether to go forward.

Common mistakes

  • Attacking the nearest goal out of habit rather than the open one.
  • All three attackers on the same side of the pitch, which makes the defenders’ job easy.
  • Failing to scan before receiving. The picture changes every time the ball turns over.
  • Letting it run too long. Three minutes flat out, then rest — tired players stop scanning, which is the one thing the game exists to coach.

What good looks like

  • Players’ heads are up before the ball reaches them.
  • The point of attack switches quickly once defenders commit to one side.
  • On losing the ball, one player delays and the other two split the two goals without being told.

Age groups and numbers

Works at every age from under-8s to open-age, which is why it is the most useful game in this library when numbers are short. For the youngest groups use larger goals and no goalkeepers. For older ones, widen the pitch so the switch is longer and the recovery runs genuinely hurt.
